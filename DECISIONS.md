script named in each entry. Newest entries at the bottom.

| D03 | 2026-10-24 | Request **only class 40 (bathymetry)** photons, per year | All classes (sea surface + unclassified + bathymetry) in one request | First attempt asked for every class: tens of millions of surface photons, 2.4 GB RAM and still running after 10 min. | D04 | 2026-10-24 | Keep photons with ensemble **confidence ≥ 0.6** (`low_confidence_flag == 0`) and `sensor_depth_exceeded == 0` | No filter; stricter 0.8 | 0.6 is the threshold NSIDC itself uses to define `low_confidence_flag`. Stricter thresholds remove mostly the deeper (rarer, more valuable) returns. Remaining noise is handled by pixel aggregation (D06). | 862,202 → 784,213 photons (`src/prepare_atl24.py`). |
| D05 | 2026-10-24 | Label = **tide-free depth below local mean sea level**: `MSL − ortho_h`, with MSL = median sea-surface height over all passes (EGM08) | ATL24 instantaneous depth `surface_h − ortho_h`; external tide model (FES2014/TPXO) | Instantaneous depth contains the tide at the pass time. Here the pass water level ranges −1.21 to +0.81 m around MSL, while the median depth is only 1.5 m, so tide is a huge share of the label. The seafloor height does not change with tide; averaging ~95 passes at random tide phases estimates MSL directly from the data, without an extra model. Gafoor et al. (2022) found tide to be the main error source for Abu Dhabi SDB. | Repeat passes over the same pixel (10,561 pairs) now disagree by median 0.10 m instead of 0.34 m (RMS 0.43 vs 0.61 m) (`src/check_tide_consistency.py`). MSL = +0.461 m above EGM08. |
| D06 | 2026-10-24 | Aggregate photons into the **Sentinel-2 10 m pixel grid per pass**: median depth, ≥ 3 photons, MAD ≤ 0.5 m | Use every photon as a sample; aggregate to 20 m or 30 m | The label must live on the same grid as the features. Median + minimum count + spread limit throws away isolated false photons (e.g. "deep" photons classified on the mainland at ~54.7°E). One sample per pass and pixel keeps passes independent for the split. | 192,775 pixel-pass cells → 97,730 samples (88,894 unique pixels), depth 0.02–28.5 m, median 1.48 m. |
| D07 | 2026-10-24 | Use the **Sentinel-2 Collection-1** archive (`sentinel-2-c1-l2a` on Earth Search) | The older `sentinel-2-l2a` collection; Copernicus Data Space | The older collection mixes processing baselines: a 2021 scene (PB 02.14) had water ~5× brighter in blue than a 2026 one (PB 05.13). Collection-1 reprocesses 2019–2026 with baseline 05.xx, so scenes can be compared and composited. Earth Search serves cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs with no account. | 470 scenes < 5 % cloud on 40RBN in Collection-1. |
| D08 | 2026-10-24 | Convert DN → reflectance with the **scale/offset in each asset's metadata** | Hard-code `DN/10000 − 0.1` | Hard-coding gave negative reflectance (−0.08 in SWIR over water) on the old collection, whose DNs were already offset-corrected (`earthsearch:boa_offset_applied = True`). Reading the metadata is correct for both. | Water reflectance now plausible: blue ≈ 0.05, SWIR ≈ 0.01–0.02. |
| D09 | 2026-10-24 | Backup reference = **GMRT grid (GEBCO-based in this area)**, used only as a regional sanity check | GEBCO 2024 download portal; nautical charts (not open) | Open, no login, scriptable. | D10 | 2026-10-24 | Score all 308 Collection-1 scenes (2019–2024, < 5 % cloud) on 80 m COG overviews, **resumable** (one CSV line per scene) | Trust the catalogue cloud % only; score at full resolution | Catalogue cloud % says nothing about glint or turbidity. Overviews cost ~1 MB per scene. The first (non-resumable) run was killed by low system memory after ~40 min and lost everything, so the script now appends each result immediately and resumes. | `data/interim/scene_scores.csv`, 308 scenes. |
| D11 | 2026-10-24 | Pick the **10 best scenes** by rank-sum of glint (median B11 over water) and turbidity (p75 red over water), requiring < 1 % cloud in the covered area and the normal coverage (no-data ≤ 15 %) | Best single scene; all clear scenes; seasonal subset | "Full coverage" is impossible on 40RBN (every scene has ≥ 10 % no-data at the swath edge), so the filter had to be relative. All 10 selected scenes are winter (Nov–Mar): low sun → less glint, calmer and clearer water, which matches the physics. | 90 eligible scenes; chosen list in `data/interim/chosen_scenes.csv`. |
| D12 | 2026-10-24 | **Glint removal with SWIR-1 (B11)**: R_corr = R − R_B11 per scene | Hedley et al. 2005 NIR regression; no correction | Pure-water penetration (1/a): NIR ~0.23 m, SWIR-1 ~1.5 mm (Hale & Querry 1973). Most labels are < 2 m over bright sand where NIR still carries bottom signal, so NIR-based removal would subtract depth information. SWIR is black over any water. | Water reflectance after correction: B02 median 0.048, B03 0.045; 2–4 % of mixed shoreline pixels go slightly negative. |
| D13 | 2026-10-24 | **Per-pixel median composite** of the 10 corrected scenes (+ a single-scene version to measure the gain) | Best single scene; mean composite | Median is robust to residual glint, boats, haze and the tide state of one date. Tested on identical pixels. | RMSE composite vs single: RF 0.996 vs 1.085 m; Stumpf 1.93 vs 2.44 m (92,493 common samples; `results/summary.json`). Composite wins for every model. |
| D14 | 2026-10-24 | Water mask = MNDWI (G, SWIR1) > 0, ≥ 3 clear scenes, **connected water body ≥ 1,000 px (0.1 km²)** | NDWI with NIR; SCL water class; MNDWI alone | NIR-based NDWI fails over very shallow bright water. MNDWI alone marked ~60,000 speckles of wet sabkha inland as water. The size filter keeps 18 real water bodies and removes the speckle. | Labels lost to the filter: ~100 (of 97,730). |
| D15 | 2026-10-24 | Lyzenga R_deep = **5th percentile** of water reflectance | 0.5th percentile (first try); a manual deep-water polygon | 0.5th percentile picked the slightly negative shoreline pixels (R_deep < 0 is not physical). The AOI has no truly optically deep water, so a robust low percentile is the practical stand-in. | R_deep: B01 0.006, B02 0.017, B03 0.023, B04 0.0009. |
| D16 | 2026-10-24 | **Validity rule for the log-ratio**: blue and green > 0.001 (ln(1000·R) > 0); applied to **all** models so they are scored on the same samples. Red/coastal ratios set to NaN where undefined | Clip to a floor value; keep all pixels | First Stumpf run had R² ≈ 0 although Spearman ρ with depth was 0.69 (blue/green) and 0.84 (blue/red): a handful of near-zero reflectances produced ratios of −16 to +19 and broke the 2-parameter fit. The rule is part of the method's definition, not tuning. | 459 samples removed (0.5 %). Stumpf R² 0 → 0.42 (track split). |
| D17 | 2026-10-24 | Add a **switching log-ratio model** (blue/red in very shallow water, blue/green deeper, blend 2–4 m; red used only where red > 0.01) | Plain Stumpf only | Our per-bin medians show the blue/red ratio rising 1.09 → 1.80 from 0 to 5 m then saturating, as Caballero & Stumpf (2020) describe. First version without the red > 0.01 rule got worse (bias −0.82 m): red just above 0.001 still explodes. | 0–5 m RMSE 1.49 (Stumpf) → 0.87 m (switching). A fair, stronger classical baseline. |
| D18 | 2026-10-24 | Models: **Random Forest and XGBoost** on 12 features (5 bands, 3 Stumpf ratios, 4 Lyzenga terms); **no coordinates**; fixed, literature-typical hyper-parameters | CNN/U-Net; geospatial foundation model; SVR; tuned XGBoost | Pixel-wise tabular problem with ~96k samples: tree ensembles are the strong default and used in SDB literature (Sagawa 2019, Gafoor 2022). Dense-label deep models do not fit line-shaped ICESat-2 labels. Coordinates would let the model memorise neighbourhoods. Hyper-parameters fixed a priori because tuning inside a 5-fold spatial CV would need nested CV (time); RF is robust to them. | RF 300 trees, min_samples_leaf 5, max_features 0.5; XGB 800 trees, lr 0.03, depth 6. |
| D19 | 2026-10-24 | **Primary evaluation = leave-one-RGT-out + 1 km buffer** (all cycles of a ground track held out, training pixels within 1 km of any test pixel removed) | Random K-fold; GroupKFold by pass; by pass + 500 m buffer | Measured the leakage directly. RF RMSE: random 0.71 → by pass 0.79 → pass + 500 m 0.86 → RGT + 500 m 0.91 → RGT + 1 km 0.99 m. A 500 m buffer around single passes deletes 2/3 of training data (tracks interleave densely), so holding out whole RGTs is both stricter and keeps more data. Buffer sweep 0/250/500/1000/2000 m: RF error plateaus from ~1 km (0.99 → 1.00). The variogram keeps rising to 5 km (broad banks), so no buffer removes all dependence; beyond 1 km we mostly lose data. RF ≈ 0.5–0.57 × baseline error at every buffer, so the conclusion does not depend on the choice. | `results/metrics_*.csv`, `buffer_sensitivity.csv`, `variogram.csv`, `figures/leakage.png`. |
